1.4 Purpose of This Document
This Business Requirements Document (BRD) defines the business need, scope, functional and non-functional requirements for a custom software platform that will replace the client’s current Excel-and-register based method of tracking jewellery manufacturing, raw material movement, karigar (craftsman) labour and product costing. 2. Executive Summary
The client is a jewellery manufacturing business that designs and produces gold jewellery (primarily 24K and 18K) set with polki and coloured stones, executed through a network of in-house and external karigars (craftsmen). Today, the entire operation — product serial numbering, raw material issue, gold dust and wastage reconciliation, karigar labour entry, and final product costing — is run on a combination of a single Excel estimate workbook and handwritten register copies.
This approach has carried the business so far, but it has reached its structural limits. There is no single source of truth for how much gold was issued against a job and how much came back; wastage and dust recovery are estimated from memory rather than measured; labour payable to each karigar is reconstructed at month-end from registers; and there is no reliable way to look up an old design. Product knowledge lives in people’s heads and in loose photographs, not in a system.
This document proposes a **web-based Jewellery Manufacturing, Costing & Karigar Management System** that digitises the complete lifecycle from design sketch to finished, costed product. Every product receives a permanent serial number. Every gram of gold issued to a karigar is tracked to its return, with dust and wastage reconciled against configurable tolerances. Every labour entry is captured against the job at the moment work is booked, so karigar payables are always current. The costing engine replicates the client’s existing Excel formulas exactly — including the derivation of the 18K rate as 75% of the 24K rate — so that finance staff see numbers they already trust, computed automatically rather than by hand.
The platform also introduces a capability the client cannot achieve with Excel at all: **AI-based design image search**. A staff member photographs a physical piece, or uploads a customer-supplied design image, and the system returns the closest matching products from the catalogue along with their serial numbers, specifications and past costings. What is currently a twenty-minute hunt through registers and photo folders — often ending in failure — becomes a five-second lookup.

3. Business Background & Current State Analysis
3.1 About the Client’s Business
The client manufactures gold jewellery, working principally in 24K (pure) and 18K gold, with polki (uncut diamond) and coloured stone settings. Manufacturing is performed by karigars — skilled craftsmen — who receive raw gold and stones against a job, perform their stage of work (casting, filing, setting, polishing, and so on), and return the piece along with any recovered gold dust. The business handles both made-to-order customer pieces and repeat production of established designs.
3.2 As-Is Process
The current end-to-end process, as observed during discovery, runs as follows:
A design is agreed with the customer, typically from a hand-drawn sketch or a photograph of an existing piece.
A serial number is written into a register copy by hand. Sequence is maintained by whoever is holding the register.
A rough estimate is prepared in the Excel workbook “Normal Detail 24-18 K.xlsx” to quote the customer.
Raw gold is weighed and issued to a karigar. The weight is noted in the register.
The karigar performs the work. Gold dust generated during filing and polishing is collected and returned separately, in principle.
The finished piece is weighed on return. The difference between issue and receipt is treated as wastage, absorbed informally.
Labour is agreed and noted — sometimes per gram, sometimes per piece, sometimes verbally — and settled at month-end.
The final costing is completed in Excel, profit is added as a percentage, and the net amount is quoted or invoiced.
The photograph of the finished piece, if taken at all, is stored on a phone or in an unstructured folder.

3.3.3 Structural Limitations Identified in the Workbook
Fixed line-item capacity. Only 8 polki rows and 5 coloured stone rows exist. A piece with more stone varieties cannot be costed without breaking the formulas.
Purity factor is buried in a formula. The 75% conversion sits inside cell G6. If the client ever needs 22K (91.66%) or 14K (58.5%), the sheet must be rebuilt.
No rate history. The gold rate typed in on the day of the estimate is overwritten by the next estimate. There is no way to reconstruct what rate a past job was costed at.
No serial number linkage. The sheet has an “S.no.” column, but it numbers the cost heads (1–4), not the product. The product serial number lives only in the register.
Nothing is captured on the input side. The sheet records the cost of gold consumed, but not the gold issued, returned, lost as wastage, or recovered as dust.
No karigar attribution. Making charges appear as a single line. Which karigar earned it, and for what stage of work, is not recorded.
Single file, single user. The workbook cannot be safely used by two people at once, has no audit trail, and one accidental overwrite destroys history.
Totals are brittle. H16 and H22 reference individual cells by name. Inserting a row silently excludes it from the total — a class of error that produces a plausible but wrong number.

4. Project Objectives & Success Criteria
4.1 Business Objectives
Eliminate dependency on Excel and register copies for all manufacturing, material, labour and costing records, replacing them with a single authoritative system of record.
Establish end-to-end metal accountability, so that for every job the gold issued, returned, recovered as dust and lost as wastage is known, measured and reconciled.
Automate product costing using the client’s existing formulas, with rates, purity factors and profit margins driven from configurable masters rather than hand-typed cells.
Capture karigar labour at source, at the moment work is booked against a job, producing a live and defensible payables position.
Preserve design intellectual property by attaching sketches and finished-product photographs permanently to every serial-numbered product.
Enable instant design retrieval by image, allowing staff to identify a product and its serial number from a photograph alone.
Give management real-time visibility through dashboards and reports covering work-in-progress, metal position, wastage, labour cost and margin.
Enforce accountability through role-based access control and an immutable audit trail on every financially material transaction.

Master data management — gold rates, purity/karat definitions, stone types, product categories, karigars, employees, customers and vendors.

7.2 Metal Reconciliation Logic
The reconciliation performed at Step 9 is the financial heart of the system and is stated formally here:
**Net Wastage (g) = Gold Issued (g) − Finished Piece Weight (g) − Gold Dust Recovered (g) − Gold Returned Unused (g)**
**Wastage % = (Net Wastage ÷ Gold Issued) × 100**
Where all weights are normalised to fine gold (i.e. gross weight × purity factor) before comparison, so that gold issued at one purity and returned at another reconciles correctly. The resulting Wastage % is compared against the tolerance configured for the process stage and karigar category. Wastage within tolerance is absorbed into product cost automatically. Wastage beyond tolerance raises an exception that must be approved by a Manager with a recorded reason, and can optionally be recovered from the karigar’s labour payable.

9. Business Rules
Business rules constrain system behaviour independently of any single screen or module. They are stated here once and referenced throughout design and testing.
Rule ID
Business Rule
BR-01
Purity factors. 24K = 0.995 (or 1.000, configurable), 22K = 0.9166, 18K = 0.750, 14K = 0.585. All gold weights are converted to fine gold using these factors before any reconciliation.
BR-02
Derived rate. The rate for any purity below 24K defaults to `24K rate × purity factor`. For 18K this yields the client’s existing 75% derivation. A manual override is permitted only for the Costing and Manager roles, and is recorded in the audit trail.
BR-03
Rate immutability. A gold or stone rate, once referenced by an approved estimate, is frozen against that estimate. Changing the master rate never alters a historical estimate.
BR-04
Line amount. Gold line amount = weight (g) × rate per gram. Stone line amount = carat × rate per carat. There are no other amount formulas.
BR-05
Cost build-up. Cost = Gold total + Polki total + Coloured Stone total + Making Charges + Other Charges + Wastage Cost.
BR-06
Profit. Profit = Cost × Profit % ÷ 100. Net Amount = Cost + Profit. Profit percentage is never negative.
BR-07
Serial number. Auto-generated, unique, immutable and never reused, including when a product is cancelled, melted or scrapped.
BR-08
Estimate locking. An estimate in Approved status cannot be edited. Any change creates a new version and marks the prior version Superseded.
BR-09
Wastage. Net Wastage = Fine gold issued − fine gold in piece − fine gold in dust − fine gold returned. Wastage % = Net Wastage ÷ Fine gold issued × 100.
BR-10
Wastage tolerance. Wastage within the tolerance configured for the stage is absorbed automatically. Wastage above tolerance blocks stage approval until a Manager approves it with a recorded reason.
BR-11
Dust recovery. Dust is credited to inventory only at the actual pure gold recovered from the refiner, never at the nominal dust weight.
BR-12
Job closure. A job card cannot be closed while any material is unreconciled, any wastage exception is unresolved, or any labour entry is unapproved.
BR-13
Transaction immutability. Posted material and labour transactions are never edited or deleted. Corrections are made by an explicit reversing entry that references the original.
BR-14
Negative stock. A material issue that would drive stock negative is blocked, unless a Manager overrides it with a recorded reason.
BR-15
Rounding. Weights and carats are stored and displayed to three decimal places. Currency is computed to two decimal places. Rounding is applied at presentation, never mid-calculation, so that totals always equal the sum of their displayed lines.
BR-16
Data visibility. Cost, rate, margin and profit values are never rendered — in any screen, export, print or API response — to the Store, Production, Sales or Karigar roles.
BR-17
Visual search confidence. Where the highest similarity score is below the configured threshold, the system reports “no confident match” rather than presenting the nearest result as an identification.
BR-18
Master deactivation. A master record referenced by any transaction can be deactivated but never deleted.

12. Visual Search — Technical Approach & Accuracy Expectations
This section is written for the client’s benefit as much as the delivery team’s. Image-based product search is the most technically demanding element of this scope and the one most vulnerable to misunderstood expectations. It is described here plainly so that what is being bought is unambiguous at sign-off.
12.1 How It Will Work
Indexing. Every product image uploaded to the system is passed through a pre-trained deep-learning vision model, which converts the picture into a numerical vector — an “embedding” — of several hundred dimensions. This vector encodes the visual character of the piece: its silhouette, motif density, stone layout and texture.
Storage. These vectors are stored in a vector index alongside the product’s serial number. Indexing happens automatically and incrementally on upload (FR-9.10); there is no manual retraining step for the client.
Querying. When a staff member uploads a query photograph, it is pre-processed — background removed, orientation corrected, normalised — and converted into a vector by the same model.
Matching. The system computes the similarity between the query vector and every indexed vector, and returns the closest matches ranked by score, each with its serial number and specifications.
Refinement. Structured filters (category, purity, weight range) can be combined with the visual query to narrow the candidate set dramatically, which is the single most effective lever on real-world precision (FR-9.05).
Feedback. When a user confirms which returned result was correct, that signal is recorded and used to tune ranking over time (FR-9.09).
12.2 What the Client Must Understand
**Visual search returns ranked probabilities, not certainties.** The system does not “know” which piece is in the photograph. It reports which indexed products look most like it, with a confidence score. In the overwhelming majority of cases with a reasonable photograph, the correct product will appear in the top few results. It will not always be the first result, and occasionally — with a poor photograph, or a design that closely resembles several others — it may not appear at all.
This is a property of the technology, not a defect in the implementation. It is why FR-9.02 specifies a **ranked list of five results** rather than a single answer, and why FR-9.08 requires the system to say “no confident match found” rather than assert a weak match. A system that always returns exactly one answer would be more satisfying and considerably less trustworthy.
12.3 Factors That Determine Accuracy
Factor
Effect on Accuracy
Mitigation
Catalogue image quality
The single largest driver. Consistent lighting, plain background and a standard angle dramatically improve results.

Combine visual similarity with structured filters (FR-9.05). Present the top five, not the top one, and let the human decide.

A blurry, angled photograph under yellow shop lighting will retrieve less reliably than a clean one.

Sketch vs finished piece
A pencil sketch and a polished gold piece are visually very different objects. Cross-modal matching is intrinsically harder.
Index sketches and finished photographs in the same space and match against both (FR-9.07). Expect lower accuracy on sketch queries than on photograph queries.
